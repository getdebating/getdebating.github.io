Debate drills

Drills you can run yourself

Short exercises for any classroom. No equipment needed.

One-minute speeches

  • 15 minutes
  • Whole class, in pairs

Practises: speaking without notes.

  1. Put students in pairs. Give every pair an easy topic, such as "the best day of the week".
  2. Partner A speaks for one minute without stopping. Partner B listens and counts every "um" and "like".
  3. Swap roles with a new topic.
  4. Ask a few pairs what made a speech easy to follow.
  5. Go again with a harder topic.

The why chain

  • 10 minutes
  • Pairs

Practises: explaining a reason in full.

  1. Give each pair a simple claim, such as "school should start later".
  2. Partner A states the claim and gives one reason.
  3. Partner B asks "Why?". A answers. B asks again, five times in a row.
  4. Swap roles with a new claim.
  5. Each pair shares the deepest reason they reached.

Rebuttal tennis

  • 10 minutes
  • Pairs

Practises: answering the other side.

  1. Give the class a motion. In each pair, A argues for and B argues against.
  2. A makes one short point.
  3. B must answer that point before making a new one: "They said… but…".
  4. Keep the rally going. Whoever repeats a point or stalls for five seconds loses the rally.
  5. Swap sides and play again.

Where do you stand?

  • 15 minutes
  • Whole class

Practises: forming an opinion and defending it.

  1. Name one wall "Agree" and the opposite wall "Disagree".
  2. Read out a statement. Students stand anywhere along the line between the walls.
  3. Ask two or three students in different spots to explain why they are standing there.
  4. Invite anyone who has been persuaded to move, and to say what changed their mind.
  5. Repeat with a new statement.

Build a point

  • 20 minutes
  • Groups of 3 or 4

Practises: structuring an argument.

  1. Write four words on the board: Point, Reason, Example, Link.
  2. Give each group a motion and a side.
  3. Groups write one argument in four sentences, one for each word. The last sentence links back to the motion.
  4. One speaker from each group delivers it.
  5. The class says which of the four parts was strongest and which needs work.

Speed prep

  • 25 minutes
  • Teams of 3 or 4

Practises: preparing quickly.

  1. Split the class into teams. Half are Proposition, half are Opposition.
  2. Reveal a motion and start a five-minute timer.
  3. Each team agrees its three best points and who will say each one.
  4. Every speaker gets 30 seconds to deliver one point.
  5. Finish by asking: what would you have done with five more minutes?
